A Doodle alternative built for board game nights

Updated 17 November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

Doodle, Rallly and chat polls are built to find a date. A game group also needs to agree on what to play and whether enough players can come. MeppleTime runs the date poll the same way (one link, no accounts for voters, yes, maybe or no on each date) and adds game voting with BoardGameGeek search and player counts. It is free, with no ads and no paid tier.

If your group already uses Doodle or a poll in the group chat, you know the routine: someone lists dates, everyone ticks boxes, and the real questions start afterwards. What are we playing? Can six people play it? Who is actually coming? This page compares the common options for that job, based on each tool's own documentation.

What a game night poll needs

  • Date voting without sign-ups. Friends will not create an account to answer one question.
  • A "maybe". Game nights often hinge on one or two people who are not sure yet.
  • The game choice in the same place. The date and the game decide each other.
  • Player counts. A great turnout is a problem for a four-player game.
  • A clear final answer. One date, one game, and a list of who is coming.

Doodle

Doodle is the name most people know, and its group poll handles the date part well: participants vote without an account and can answer "if need be". For a regular group the free plan is narrow, though. Doodle's plan page lists one group poll on the free plan, and its help center marks voting deadlines, participant limits and reminders as paid Professional features. An ad-free experience is also listed as a paid benefit. There is no built-in way to choose a game or check player counts.

Choose Doodle if you already pay for it at work, or you need time slots within a day rather than whole days.

Rallly

Rallly is a clean, open-source scheduling tool (AGPL-3.0). Participants vote without an account and can answer "if need be", and the free plan does not limit the number of participants. Two limits matter for a game group: only three polls can collect votes at the same time, and free polls are deleted once their dates are more than 30 days past and there has been no activity for 30 days. Like Doodle, it schedules dates and times, not games.

Choose Rallly if you need time slots, or you would like a tool you can host yourself.

A poll in the group chat

WhatsApp and Messenger polls are the quickest option when the group already lives in one chat. They are option polls, though: each person picks from a list, so there is no yes, maybe or no for each date, no overview of who can make which night, and choosing the game means a second poll. They work well for two or three people and an almost obvious date, and get messy beyond that.

Choose a chat poll if the group is small and the date is nearly settled.

MeppleTime

MeppleTime takes the Doodle-style date poll and adds what game nights need. Anyone in the poll can suggest games with live BoardGameGeek search and vote for every game they would enjoy; the leading game appears next to the winning date. You can set a minimum and maximum player count, so each date shows whether enough people can play, and an optional voting deadline closes the poll on its own. A group availability view shades each date by how many people can come, and a table shows everyone's answers side by side.

It is free, with no ads and no paid tier, works in English and Polish, and its code is open source (GPL-3). Two honest limits: polls offer whole days rather than time slots, and there is no calendar export.

Choose MeppleTime if you are planning a board game night and want the date, the game and the head count settled in one place.
